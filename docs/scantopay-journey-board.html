ScanPay · Build Journey

From teardown
to our own
payment gateway

How we reverse-engineered a self-hosted UPI gateway and built ScanPay — a zero-fee UPI + COD gateway that collects into your own Axis account, owns your customer data, and detects payments from your phone. Every section below: what we did, and why.

11 steps · the full journey
01

The Ask & The Teardown

What we did

We read the open-source YouPI-PG repo to learn how a self-hosted UPI gateway detects payments, then rebuilt our own clean version as one Node process instead of its 7 services.

Why

You wanted your OWN payment gateway — zero per-transaction fees, money landing straight in your Axis UPI, no Razorpay/Shiprocket cut. Studying a working teardown first meant we copied the mechanism, not the mess.

02

The Shopify Reality

What we did

Confirmed Shopify blocks custom UPI gateways in native checkout unless you are an approved payments partner. So the plan: a manual UPI method, buyer pays on the order-status page, our server auto-marks it paid via the Admin API.

Why

Better to know the one hard constraint up front than build a checkout Shopify will never allow. This keeps the native cart and still collects over UPI.

03

PG Core Engine

What we did

Built the heart of ScanPay: order state machine, dynamic UPI QR to your real Axis VPA, single-slot queue, 5-minute timer, live status over SSE, and an HMAC-SHA256 signed webhook. Proven end-to-end.

Why

This is the gateway. Everything else (CRM, app, OTP) just feeds or reads it. A strict state machine + signed webhook is what makes a payment confirmation trustworthy.

04

Why A Web CRM

What we did

A merchant needs one cockpit. We referenced your live Shiprocket Fastrr dashboard, then built our own: Overview, Orders, Customers, Settings — with a date range, Orders/Sales toggle, and a checkout conversion funnel from real events.

Why

Raw APIs don’t run a business; a dashboard does. Copying Fastrr’s proven structure (KPI cards + funnel) saved design time, and the funnel is honest — it’s computed from events we already log.

05

Rebrand To ScanPay

What we did

Applied your oxblood + bone brand system (Inter Tight, the red spark) across every surface and named the gateway ScanPay. ScanPay = the gateway product; Careatic = the store.

Why

A payment product people trust has to look deliberate and consistent. Separating the gateway name from the store keeps it reusable for clients later, not just Careatic.

06

Verified Customer Capture

What we did

Checkout now collects name, phone, email and verifies the phone by OTP before payment, with a marketing-consent checkbox. It all lands in YOUR database, and an Abandoned-carts view lets you re-engage.

Why

This is ScanPay’s edge. Shiprocket/Fastrr collects your buyers’ data and monetizes it (Engage360 WhatsApp, prefill to other merchants). Self-hosting keeps that first-party data private and yours.

07

Your Customer List

What we did

The Customers tab groups every buyer by phone, shows a Verified badge, email, order count, paid count and lifetime spend — first-party, owned by you.

Why

Owning verified customer data is the whole point: it powers your own WhatsApp/email follow-ups instead of a third party guessing “abandoned” from data you never see.

08

Real OTP Channel

What we did

Wired a pluggable OTP channel: console for the demo, real delivery over WhatsApp via Baileys (pair once by scanning a QR). SMS can be added later as a one-function plug.

Why

WhatsApp is free (fits the zero-fee ethos) and where Indian buyers already are; SMS means per-message cost and India DLT registration. Pluggable so you’re never locked to one channel.

09

Detection Layer & The Axis Finding

What we did

Built a Gmail observer + an Axis email parser, then checked your inbox: from:axisbank.com returns only card statements, EMI and no-dues — no UPI credit alerts. Axis sends those by SMS, not email.

Why

Critical: the Gmail observer can’t see your UPI collections at all. So the Axis parser’s real home is SMS, and the phone — not email — becomes the detector. This is why the Android app matters.

10

The Android App

What we did

A native Kotlin app that reads incoming bank SMS (primary) and bank-app push (fallback), parses the Axis credit, reports it to the server, and shows the CRM in a WebView with a “Payment received” push. De-duped by UTR.

Why

Since Axis only SMSes credits, your phone is the only reliable detector. Native Kotlin (not React Native) gives dependable background SMS reading; the WebView reuses the CRM we already built — one app, both jobs.

11
